# 5 Dangerous Clauses in Hospital Employment Contracts

Young consultants eagerly signing their first corporate hospital contracts often ignore the fine print. When a medical negligence lawsuit hits, they are shocked to discover the hospital management shifting the entire legal and financial burden onto them.

**Watch out for these five dangerous clauses:**

### 1. The "Sole Liability" Indemnity Clause

Look for wording that says: *"The Consultant shall indemnify the Hospital against any and all claims of negligence."* 

> **Scenario:** If a court awards ₹50 lakhs to a patient due to a nursing error in the ICU, the hospital might use this clause to recover that money entirely from you.

### 2. Ambiguous Definition of "Independent Contractor"

Hospitals often classify doctors as "independent contractors" rather than "employees" to avoid vicarious liability. 

- **Action Step:** Ensure your contract clearly defines the scope of your independence versus the hospital's responsibility for infrastructure, staffing, and equipment failures.

### 3. Forced Requirement of Personal Indemnity Coverage

While having personal indemnity is essential, some contracts force you to purchase astronomical coverages (e.g., ₹5 Crores) out of pocket, specifically naming the hospital as a co-beneficiary.

### 4. Non-Compete Clauses Post-Termination

Be wary of clauses restricting you from practicing within a 10 km radius for two years after leaving. While Indian courts often view broad non-competes as a restriction of trade, fighting them is costly and stressful.

### 5. Media & Gag Orders

In the event of a high-profile patient complication, contracts may strip you of your right to defend your clinical decisions in public or to the media, making you the hospital's scapegoat.
