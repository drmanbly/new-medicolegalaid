# First 48 Hours: How to Respond When a Legal Notice Arrives

The envelope arrives. It’s from a lawyer representing the family of a patient you treated months ago. They are alleging medical negligence and demanding ₹1 Crore in compensation.

Panic sets in. What you do in the next 48 hours will heavily dictate the outcome of the case.

### Step 1: Do Not Contact the Patient

Your instinct will be to call the patient or their family to "clear up the misunderstanding." **Do not do this.** 

> Any communication can be construed as an admission of guilt, tampering with a witness, or an attempt to intimidate. All communication must now go through legal channels.

### Step 2: Secure the Original Medical Records

Immediately secure the original case sheet, consent forms, nursing notes, and diagnostic reports. Make multiple photocopies. 

**Under no circumstances** should any subsequent additions, alterations, or "corrections" be made to the original record. Forensic analysis easily detects tampering, which instantly turns a civil negligence case into a criminal forgery case.

### Step 3: Notify Your Indemnity Insurer

Read your professional indemnity policy. Most policies have a strict clause requiring you to notify the insurer within a specified timeframe (often 7 to 14 days) of receiving a legal threat. 

- Failure to notify them promptly can result in claim rejection.

### Step 4: Consult a Medico-Legal Expert

Do not hand over the case to a general civil lawyer who usually handles property disputes. Medical negligence is a highly specialized field. You need an expert who understands both the law and the clinical medicine behind your decisions.
