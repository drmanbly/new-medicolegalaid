# Top 5 Medico-Legal Mistakes Doctors Make in Daily Practice

After consulting on hundreds of consumer court cases against doctors, a clear pattern emerges. Most doctors don't lose cases because they are bad clinicians; they lose because of administrative and documentation errors.

### 1. Failing to Label Medico-Legal Cases (MLC)

If a patient arrives with injuries from a road traffic accident, assault, or poisoning, it is an **MLC**. Many private practitioners avoid labeling it as such to avoid "police hassle." 

> **Legal Warning:** This is a criminal offense under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) for concealing evidence.

### 2. Illegible Prescriptions & Missing Details

A prescription is a legal document. Failing to write critical details has led to fatal dispensing errors by pharmacists—and the doctor is held liable. Always include:
- The Date
- The Patient's Weight (especially in pediatrics)
- Clear dosages (avoid ambiguous abbreviations like "U" for units, which can be misread as a zero)

### 3. Issuing Backdated or False Medical Certificates

Issuing a "fitness certificate" or a "sick leave certificate" without examining the patient, or backdating it as a favor, is professional misconduct. Courts take a very strict view of doctors issuing fraudulent legal documents.

### 4. Poor Discharge Summaries

A discharge summary that lacks the condition of the patient at discharge, explicit follow-up instructions, and "danger signs" to watch out for is a major liability. 

If the patient goes home, deteriorates, and dies, the family will argue they were discharged prematurely without guidance.

### 5. Delegating Critical Tasks to Unqualified Staff

Allowing a nurse to administer IV push medications without supervision, or having an unqualified assistant take consent, invokes strict vicarious liability on the treating doctor.
